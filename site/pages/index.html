Vision Pro first

It started as an SSH client for Apple Vision Pro, and the headset still leads the design. Each session is a normal window you place around you. A server's terminal, its database and its desktop can sit side by side in the room. A window you aren't using can shrink into a small glass orb, with its session live inside it, until you pinch it back.

Every window is glass. Opacity sets how much of the theme's colour is painted, Glass how much the room is frosted behind it; the pictures here use 78% and 50%.

The Mac, iPad and iPhone apps are the same app, adapted to each device. The Mac adds a local shell and menus. The iPhone gives each session the whole screen.

An honest note on testing. The current build is used every day on a Mac, an iPhone and a Vision Pro: on 2026-11-13 the Mac, the iPhone and the Vision Pro had 0.4.618, the Mac's release. Not every newer change has been checked on every device yet.

Remote desktops on Apple Vision Pro

The orb dock

Set Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Orbs ▸ Shrink to Orb to Into the Dock, and a window you shrink closes into an orb in one dock, with Connections first. A terminal window's tabs sit on a ring around its orb. A machine's databases, desktops and editors gather on that machine's orb. Pinch an orb, and its window comes back above the dock at the size it had. Resize the dock, and the orbs wrap to its shape.

A window's tabs, side by side

Terminal Overview (⌥⌘G on the Mac) shows a window's tabs as live pictures: in a grid, as Show All Windows draws them, or as a carousel, one in front and large with the rest turned toward it on either side, like an album view. Swipe, drag or press the arrow keys to go along the row, and click the one in front or press Return to open it. With Auto-focus on activity, the tab that is printing comes forward. On iPhone and iPad, Terminal View at the top left chooses Tabs, Grid or Carousel; on Vision Pro it is in the window's bar.

In the Mac's release, 0.4.618, and in the beta for iPhone, iPad and Vision Pro.

Pick up where you left off

Turn on Keep Sessions Running with tmux for a machine, and each terminal you open there runs in its own tmux session on the machine. A dropped connection, a closed window or a quit app leaves the work running. The machine needs tmux.

Every look

What it does

Four tools share one library. You add a machine once, with its address. Then you add the ways in: a terminal, a database, a desktop. The address, keys and trust you set up once are used by every tool that needs them.

❯

Terminal

SSH with host-key pinning, password and key sign-in, tabs, workgroups, port forwarding and an SFTP file browser. Sessions kept in tmux that you pick up on any device. A link or a path a terminal prints has actions of its own: open the file, preview it over the terminal, or follow a log in a new tab. A local shell on the Mac.

 ⛁

Databases

MySQL, PostgreSQL and SQLite. Browse, filter and edit rows, follow foreign keys, write and explain queries. Reach a server directly with TLS, or through the machine's own SSH connection.

 ▣

Remote desktops

RDP and VNC. The remote resolution can follow your window. Audio and a shared clipboard, with formatting and images over RDP, when you turn them on.

 ✎

Editor

Files on this device or on a server. Projects with a file tree, highlighting for fifteen languages, Git, previews for Markdown, HTML, SVG and CSV, and viewers for pictures, PDFs, video, audio and 3D models.

 ☰

The library

One list of hosts, with favorites, collections and search. It syncs through iCloud. Credentials sync through iCloud Keychain. Your Library shows the time you spend on each machine, across your devices. Widgets show your machines and what's open, and Show All Windows shows every open session as a live picture.

 ⌘

Around the system

Siri and Shortcuts, Spotlight, Controls, Handoff and ssh:// links. Send files to a machine from Shortcuts, or on the Mac from Share ▸ Send to a Machine. On the Mac, a Finder toolbar button, Services and Quick Actions open a terminal in a folder.

Why one app

A machine usually offers several ways in. The common setup is one app per protocol. Each app keeps its own list of the same machines and its own settings. Glass Power Tools keeps one list instead. Whether that fits how you work is for you to judge.

iPad and iPhone

The same library and the same machines. The iPhone gives each session the whole screen.

iPhone Duo

Half open like a small laptop, each session keeps its work above the fold and its keys below, and a remote desktop has the top half's full width. Held like a book, the inner screen is two pages, for any two sessions side by side. Both are in the beta.

What leaves your devices

The app connects to these places, and no others. The Mac app also checks its update feed on this site, and asks on its second launch whether to do that automatically.

  • The servers you add. Only when you connect to them.
  • iCloud. Your host library syncs through your private iCloud database. Passwords, keys and host-key pins sync through iCloud Keychain, except a password you protect with Face ID or Touch ID, which stays on its device. Keys made in the Secure Enclave never leave the device they were made on.
  • Tailscale's API, only if you connect a Tailscale account to find your devices.
  • A 3D model's address, only when you choose to open a model's link that a terminal printed.
  • A repository's remote, on the Mac, when you pull, push, fetch or clone in the editor: the Mac's own git reaches it, with your git settings and keys.

There are no analytics and no telemetry. Secrets live in the Keychain, apart from your settings. The database assistant runs on your device through Apple Intelligence, and the help lists exactly what it is given.

What it asks of you

On the Mac

Download 0.4.618. It is signed with a Developer ID, notarized by Apple, and updates itself through Sparkle. It is not in the Mac App Store, because a terminal cannot work inside App Sandbox: it has to start your shell and reach your files, tools and SSH agent. It needs macOS 26 on a Mac with Apple silicon.

It is early. It is used every day, and what has been checked, and what has not, is written down. Expect rough edges, and report what you find.

iPhone, iPad and Vision Pro

The iPhone, iPad and Vision Pro app is in a beta by invitation: request access with the email of your Apple Account, and the invitation comes from TestFlight. It will come to the App Store as 1.0, with the Mac at 1.0 too. It needs iOS or iPadOS 26, or visionOS 26.

You can also build any of them from source. You need a Mac with Apple silicon and Xcode 27, and you sign it under your own Apple Developer team.

Leaving

You should be able to stop using a tool without losing what you put into it. Here is what that looks like.

  • The code is free software under the GPLv3. You can read it, change it and build it yourself, and any copy someone shares stays under the same licence.
  • It speaks standard protocols: SSH, SFTP, the MySQL and PostgreSQL protocols, RDP and VNC. Your servers need nothing from this app. Any other client can reach them the same way.
  • Imported SSH keys are ordinary OpenSSH keys, the same format other SSH clients use.
  • A SQLite file is opened as a private copy. Your original is never changed.
  • Your editor files stay where they are, on your device or your server. Removing a project from the list does not touch its folder.

What you cannot take with you yet: the list of hosts and services itself has no export. If you leave, you enter it again in the next app.

And one cost that is permanent: a key made in the Secure Enclave cannot be exported. That is what makes it safe. If you leave, add a different key to the servers that use it first.
