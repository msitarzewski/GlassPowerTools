# Favorites, collections and other names

The library gives you four ways to narrow a long list of hosts: favorites, recent hosts, collections and other names. This page explains each one and which of them travel to your other devices.

## Mark a host as a favorite

Do one of these:

- Right-click the host, or touch and hold it (pinch and hold on Vision Pro), and choose **Add Favorite**.
- Select the host and choose **Add Favorite** in its details.

A yellow star appears beside the host's name, and the host is listed under **Favorites**. Favorites are ordered with the most recently connected first. To undo, choose **Remove Favorite** in the same places.

Favorites travel to your other devices: marking a host on your Mac marks it on your Vision Pro, and removing it removes it everywhere. The host for the device itself, such as **This Mac**, is the exception, because every device has its own.

## Add a service, project, workgroup or folder to Favorites

**Favorites** can also hold the things you open most, each in its own section under the hosts: **Services**, **Projects**, **Workgroups** and **Folders**.

- **A service:** select its host, then right-click the service in **Services**, or touch and hold it (pinch and hold on Vision Pro), and choose **Add Favorite**. Choosing it in **Favorites** connects to that service.
- **A project:** in **Projects**, right-click the project or touch and hold it, and choose **Add Favorite**. Choosing it opens the project.
- **A workgroup:** in **Workgroups**, right-click a saved workgroup or touch and hold it, and choose **Add Favorite**. Choosing it opens its terminals.
- **A folder on a machine:** in **Files**, go to the folder and choose the star (**Add This Folder to Favorites**) in the toolbar. Choosing it opens **Files** at that folder. Its menu also has **Open as Project**.

A click or tap on one of these opens it straight away. To take one out, right-click it in **Favorites** (or touch and hold it) and choose **Remove Favorite**, or choose the same command where you added it. These favorites travel to your other devices too. One whose service, project or workgroup is not on a device, or has not arrived yet, is not listed there, and appears once it does.

## Find hosts you used recently

**Recent** lists up to ten hosts, most recently connected first. A host appears there only after a session on it actually connects. Unlike favorites, **Recent** is kept on each device.

## Put hosts in collections

A collection is a label you give to hosts, such as `staging` or `client-a`. A host can be in several collections.

1. Edit the host: select it and choose **Edit**.
2. Open **Also known as & Collections**.
3. Click or tap the **Collections** field, where it says **Add a collection…**.
4. Type a name and press Return or type a comma. The name becomes a chip. Existing collection names are suggested as you type; choose one to add it.
5. Choose **Save**.

To take a host out of a collection, choose the × on its chip, or press Delete in the empty field to remove the last chip.

Each collection appears in the sidebar under **Collections**, with the number of hosts in it.

- **On Vision Pro:** choose the **Collections** tab, then choose a collection from the list. **Back** (‹) returns to the list of collections.
- **On Mac, iPad and iPhone:** choose the collection in the sidebar's **Collections** section. The section appears once any host belongs to a collection.

Collection names ignore case, so `Staging` and `staging` are one collection. A collection disappears when no host belongs to it. Collections are saved with the host, so they appear on all your devices.

## Give a host other names

**Also known as** holds other names for the same machine, such as a short name or a former hostname.

1. Edit the host and open **Also known as & Collections**.
2. In **Also known as**, where it says **Add a name…**, type a name and press Return or type a comma.
3. Choose **Save**.

Other names are shown in the host's details and are matched by search. They also tell the library which records describe the same machine: hosts that share a name in **Also known as**, or whose address matches it, are shown as one host.

A host can have up to 64 other names and 64 collections.
